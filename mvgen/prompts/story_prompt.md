## How the pipeline works (write for it)
1. Card scenes (`"kind": "card"`) are rendered from HTML templates in the brand's colors, fonts and logo. They carry
   every word, number, product name and logo on screen. Templates:
   - `title`: eyebrow, headline, sub. Opening or chapter title.
   - `statement`: `lines` revealed one at a time (kinetic type). Mission or turning point.
   - `feature`: eyebrow, headline, sub, optional `image` (a product screenshot the user provides).
   - `list`: headline plus up to 6 `items`.
   - `stat`: headline plus up to 4 `stats` ({value, label}). Use only numbers given in the brief.
   - `quote`: `quote` and `by`. Use only quotes given in the brief.
   - `flow`: headline plus `items` as connected steps (how it works).
   - `screen`: `image` shown in a device frame with headline. Only if the brief lists screenshots.
   - `endcard`: headline (the tagline), sub, `cta`. Always the last scene.
2. Live-action scenes (`"kind": "live_action"`) are generated by a video model, one shot each, 4, 6 or 8 seconds
   (`shot.duration`). First a keyframe still is generated and approved, then it is animated. Write `shot.action` as
   plain visual direction: who, where, what they do, expression, light. One continuous action, no cuts.
   Generated footage must contain **no readable text, captions, logos, brand names, user interfaces or real people**.
   Screens in shot show only a soft glow. Put words on cards instead.
3. Cast: invent fictional people (`cast`), describe each once (age range, look, hair, clothing), and reference them
   by id in `shot.cast` (max 3 per shot). Never name or describe a real or famous person.
4. Voice: narration lines on scenes (`voice`, each with `at` = seconds after the scene starts). A voice line plays at
   about 2.6 words per second; make sure each scene's `seconds` is at least `at` + words / 2.6 + 0.4. Lines never
   overlap; the pipeline pushes a line later if needed.
5. Music: 1 to 4 score segments (`music`), each starting at a scene id; the first starts at the first scene. Each
   prompt is an instrumental brief with length, BPM, instruments and the emotional arc with timestamps, no vocals.
   One take is at most about 3 minutes, so start a new segment before that.
6. Timing: scenes crossfade for 0.4 s. Total length ≈ sum of `seconds` − 0.4 × (scenes − 1). Hit the brief's target
   length within ±10%. Live-action scenes usually run 3 to 6 s on the timeline (trim with `trim_start`); cards 4 to
   14 s depending on how much they say.

## Craft rules
- Open on the audience's world (live action), turn to the product (cards), prove it, close on people and the brand.
- One idea per scene. Short, spoken, warm sentences. No hype, no clichés, no "revolutionize" or "game-changer".
- Use only facts, numbers, names and claims that appear in the brief. If something is missing, keep it general.
- Respect every item in the brief's `must_avoid` and the brand's rules.
- Scene ids are short slugs in order, for example `s01-open`, `s02-title`.
